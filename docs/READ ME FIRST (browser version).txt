RETRO GAME LIBRARY
Catalogue the games you own: carts, tapes, discs, boxes and digital copies.
Box art and descriptions come from the free LaunchBox Games Database.
You don't need LaunchBox installed.


WHAT YOU NEED
- Windows, Mac or Linux with Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge.
  (Firefox and Safari can't save to a folder, so the app won't work in them.)
- An internet connection to show box art.
- About 100 MB of space for the free game database file.


SETTING UP (ONE TIME, ABOUT 2 MINUTES)
1. Make a folder for your library, for example "Retro Game Library" in Documents.
   Tip: put it in Google Drive, OneDrive or Dropbox and your collection is
   backed up online automatically.
2. Put "Retro Game Library.html" in that folder.
3. Open "Retro Game Library.html" with Chrome or Edge.
   If it opens in another browser: right-click the file, choose Open with,
   and pick Chrome or Edge.
4. Step 1 on screen: click "Choose folder…" and pick the folder from step 1.
   When the browser asks, click "Edit files" / "Allow".
5. Step 2 on screen: click "Download Metadata.zip". This downloads the
   LaunchBox Games Database. When it has finished, click
   "Choose Metadata.zip…" and pick the file from your Downloads folder.
   (You can skip this, but then you'll add pictures by hand.)
6. Click "Open my library".

Next time, just open the file and click "Open my library". Your browser may
ask you to allow access to the folder again.


ADDING A GAME
1. Click "+ Add game".
2. Pick the system from the list.
3. Start typing the title. Matching games appear with their box art.
   Pick yours and the cover, description, year and publisher fill in by
   themselves. If you type the full title and save, it's looked up for you.
4. Set what you have (loose, boxed, complete in box, sealed
   or digital), the condition
   and any notes, then click "Save game".

Bought it digitally (GOG, Steam, eShop and so on)? Choose "Digital" under
"What you have" and pick the store.

If a game isn't in the database, save it anyway and drop your own photo of
the box onto the picture area.
No box art? Open the game and click "Look up box art" to pick from the
matching games in the databases, or "Find online…" to search the web. Right-click the
picture you want, choose "Copy image", then press Ctrl+V (Cmd+V on a Mac)
in the app. You can also drag a picture straight from a web page.
ZX Spectrum and ZX81: rare games LaunchBox doesn't have are also looked up
in ZXDB, the Spectrum community's database, with cover scans and loading
screens.

Got two copies? Open the game and click "+ Another copy".
Own it on other machines too? Before saving, pick each extra system under
"Also own it on" and click "Add system". Each gets its own entry and box art.
Found another version later? Open the game and click "+ Same game, another system".
Use the drop-down next to the search box to show only duplicates, games on
several systems, physical or digital games only, or games missing box art.


HARDWARE
Click "Hardware" at the top to keep track of your consoles, computers,
handhelds, controllers and accessories: model and version, colour or
edition, region, serial number, boxed or not, condition, whether it works,
and any mods or repairs. Add a photo of your own machine (drop, paste or
"Choose picture…"). Filter by type or by "Needs repair or not working".
Hardware is saved in library.json with your games, gets its own
"Retro Game Library hardware.csv", its own sheet in the Excel export and
its own section in the PDF.

IMPORTING FROM A SPREADSHEET
Already have your collection in a spreadsheet? Open "Backup & tools" and,
under "Import games", click "Template (Excel)…" to get a ready-made sheet
with drop-down lists. Put one game per row (only Title and System are
needed), save it, then click "Import spreadsheet…". Excel (.xlsx) and CSV
both work, and so do most spreadsheets with a Title and System column.
Short system names are fine: Spectrum, C64, Mega Drive, SNES, PS2 and so on.
Box art and descriptions are looked up as the games are added (using the
first match), so check any you're unsure of with "Look up box art".

BACKUPS
Every change is saved straight away into your library folder:
- library.json                 your full collection
- Retro Game Library.csv       the same list as a spreadsheet (Excel, Google Sheets)
- covers                       pictures you added yourself
- backups                      a dated copy for each day you make changes
- Retro Game Library hardware.csv   your hardware list (once you add some)

To restore, open "Backup & tools" and choose a file from the backups folder.
Under "Export" you can also save a PDF catalogue, an Excel workbook or a CSV.
(In the browser version, box art that is only shown from the internet appears
as a placeholder in the PDF. The desktop apps save every cover, so their PDFs
have all the pictures.)


KEEPING THE DATABASE UP TO DATE
New games are added to the LaunchBox Games Database all the time. Now and
then, open "Backup & tools", click "Download latest", then "Choose database
file…" and pick the new Metadata.zip.
"Fill in missing covers & descriptions" then updates any games that were
added before you had the database, or that weren't in it yet.


MOVING TO A NEW PC
Copy your library folder across (or let Google Drive / OneDrive sync it),
open "Retro Game Library.html" and choose that folder in step 1.


QUESTIONS PEOPLE ASK

How do I make sure my collection is backed up to Google Drive or OneDrive?
  Install Google Drive for desktop or OneDrive (OneDrive comes with Windows).
  Then make your library folder inside the synced folder, for example
  "G:\My Drive\Retro Game Library" or "C:\Users\you\OneDrive\Retro Game Library",
  and choose that folder in step 1. The app saves to the folder and the
  Drive/OneDrive app uploads every change for you. The top of the app shows
  which folder it is "Saving to".

Can I move my library to another folder later?
  Yes. Click "Saving to …" at the top, then "Choose a different folder…".
  If the new folder is empty, your whole library (including your own
  pictures) is copied into it.

How do I know if there's a newer game database?
  LaunchBox updates its database every day. When your copy is more than
  30 days old, the app shows a reminder with a "Download latest" button.

Does the database download every time?
  No. You download Metadata.zip once and the app reads it from your PC.
  Only download it again when you want newer games.

Are the covers stored on my PC?
  Pictures you add yourself are saved in your library folder. Box art from
  the database is shown straight from LaunchBox's picture server, and your
  browser keeps a cached copy, so it needs the internet the first time it's
  shown. (Browsers don't let a web page save pictures from another website,
  which is why it can't simply download them.)

I have LaunchBox. Can the covers be saved on my PC?
  Yes. Open "Backup & tools" and click "Connect LaunchBox Images folder…",
  then pick LaunchBox's Images folder (for example F:\LaunchBox\Images).
  From then on, any cover LaunchBox has already downloaded is copied into
  your library folder when you add a game, so it works offline and is
  backed up with everything else. "Copy covers to my library folder" does
  the same for games you've already added.


PRIVACY
Everything stays on your computer. The app has no account, no server and
no tracking. It only goes online to show box art from LaunchBox's picture
server.


ABOUT
Retro Game Library is for cataloguing the games you own. It isn't
a game launcher and is in no way a replacement for LaunchBox, which is
brilliant for organising and playing your games on PC. Check it out at
launchbox-app.com.
