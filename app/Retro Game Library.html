Backup & tools

Automatic backup

Every change is saved straight away into : library.json, a spreadsheet Retro Game Library.csv, any pictures you added yourself, and a dated copy each day in backups. If that folder is inside your Google Drive, OneDrive or Dropbox folder, their app uploads every change, so it's backed up online. To move it, click Change setup below and pick a new folder: your library is copied across.

Import games

Got your collection in a spreadsheet already? Download the template, put one game per row (only Title and System are needed) and import it. Excel (.xlsx) and CSV both work, and so do most spreadsheets with a Title and System column. Box art and descriptions are looked up for you; change any of them later with Look up box art.

Export

Save a copy to keep, print, share or put on your phone. The PDF is a catalogue with box art, grouped by system, so you can flick through your collection offline.

Keep covers on your PC (LaunchBox owners)

Covers on your PC

Box art is saved into your library folder automatically, so it works offline and is backed up with everything else. If any covers are still showing from the internet (for example, added while offline), this saves them too.

If you have LaunchBox, connect its Images folder. Covers found there are copied into your library folder, so they work offline and are backed up with everything else. Other covers still show from the internet.

Restore from a backup

Choose a library-….json file from the backups folder. Games in it are added back or updated. Nothing else is removed.

About Retro Game Library

Retro Game Library is for cataloguing the games you own: carts, tapes, discs, boxes and digital copies. It isn't a game launcher and doesn't play anything.

It's in no way a replacement for LaunchBox, which is brilliant for organising and playing your games on PC. If you haven't tried it, go and check it out at launchbox-app.com.

Game information and box art come from the LaunchBox Games Database, built by the LaunchBox community. This app isn't made by, or affiliated with, LaunchBox.

For the ZX Spectrum and ZX81, games LaunchBox doesn't have are looked up in ZXDB, the open Spectrum database, through ZXInfo and Spectrum Computing. Thanks to everyone who keeps it going.
